Frequently Asked Questions

What companies ask about scope

Is the review an audit?

No. It reads operative documents for transaction readiness. It is not an audit, tax return preparation, financial statement assurance, or regulator-facing representation.

Which documents does the review read?

The operative record across the three domains: governance and corporate records, contracts, employment documentation, capitalization and financing records, operating results, and the documentation behind your software, hardware and data.

Do you prepare or check our tax returns?

No. The financial review considers whether federal and state filings, including sales tax, are current. It does not review the content or accuracy of the returns themselves.

Will you represent us before regulators?

No. The legal review considers required licenses and related records, but it does not include representation before regulators.

Can the review cover only one domain?

Scope is set at the start of an engagement. Most companies are reviewed across all three domains, because friction in one usually shows up in another.

What happens after the review?

You receive the issues found, ranked by how likely they are to create friction during diligence, so they can be resolved before a counterparty finds them.
